1. Overview

The simulator combines four standard building blocks:

  • A partial differential equation (PDE) system: the 2-D depth-averaged shallow-water equations.
  • Finite-difference / finite-volume discretization on a staggered grid.
  • Explicit time stepping with a stability-limited step size.
  • Advection–reaction equations for pollutants and ordinary / stochastic differential equations for drifting particles.

The model is never told what the tide inside the gulf looks like. It is only given the real sea level at the open-sea edge, and everything else is computed from the physics: the amplification towards Bhavnagar, the fast currents past Dahej, the eddies, and where pollution goes.

3. Governing equations: 2-D shallow-water equations

Unknowns: free-surface elevation \(\eta(x,y,t)\) and depth-averaged velocities \(u, v\). With still-water depth \(h\) from GEBCO, the total water depth is \(H = h + \eta\).

Mass conservation (continuity)
$$\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (Hu)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (Hv)}{\partial y} = 0$$
Momentum (x)
$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} - f v = -g\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} - \frac{C_d\,|\mathbf{U}|\,u}{H} + A_h \nabla^2 u + \frac{\tau_x}{\rho H}$$
Momentum (y)
$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + f u = -g\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial y} - \frac{C_d\,|\mathbf{U}|\,v}{H} + A_h \nabla^2 v + \frac{\tau_y}{\rho H}$$
TermPhysical meaningValue used
\(u\,\partial_x u + v\,\partial_y u\)Nonlinear advection of momentum; creates eddies and residual loopstoggle in UI
\(f v\), \(f = 2\Omega\sin\varphi\)Coriolis acceleration from Earth's rotation\(f \approx 5.2\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)
\(-g\,\partial_x \eta\)Pressure gradient: water accelerates down the surface slope\(g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
\(C_d |\mathbf U| u / H\)Quadratic bottom friction, \(|\mathbf U| = \sqrt{u^2+v^2}\)\(C_d = 0.0012\) (calibrated)
\(A_h \nabla^2 u\)Horizontal eddy viscosity\(A_h = 10\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}\)
\(\tau/(\rho H)\)Wind stress, \(\boldsymbol\tau = \rho_a C_{D,w} |\mathbf W| \mathbf W\)\(\rho_a = 1.22\), \(C_{D,w} = 1.3\times10^{-3}\), \(\rho = 1025\)

These equations are the Navier–Stokes equations averaged over depth, using the hydrostatic, long-wave approximation. The approximation holds here because the gulf is ~100 km long but only ~5–70 m deep, so horizontal scales are far larger than vertical ones.

4. Spatial discretization: the Arakawa C-grid

The variables are staggered: \(\eta\) sits at cell centres, \(u\) on east faces and \(v\) on north faces.

Each face's pressure gradient is then a compact centred difference between its two neighbouring cells:

$$\left.\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x}\right|_{u_{i,j}} \approx \frac{\eta_{i+1,j} - \eta_{i,j}}{\Delta x_j}$$

This avoids the checkerboard (odd–even decoupling) instability of non-staggered grids. \(\Delta x_j\) varies with latitude (dxRow[j]) because meridians converge.

5. Time integration: forward–backward explicit scheme

Each step, step() in model.js, advances \(\Delta t = 33\) s:

  1. Momentum, using the old \(\eta\), with friction treated semi-implicitly: $$u^{*} = u^n + \Delta t\left[-g\,\partial_x\eta^n + f\bar v - \text{adv} + A_h\nabla^2 u + \frac{\tau_x}{\rho H}\right],\qquad u^{n+1} = \frac{u^{*}}{1 + \Delta t\, C_d |\mathbf U| / H}$$ Dividing instead of subtracting keeps friction stable even when \(H \to 0.1\) m on drying flats, where an explicit friction term would blow up.
  2. Continuity as a finite volume, using the new velocities. This is the "forward–backward" part, which allows a larger stable \(\Delta t\): $$F^u_{i,j} = u^{n+1}_{i,j}\, H_{\text{upwind}}\, \Delta y, \qquad \eta^{n+1}_{i,j} = \eta^n_{i,j} - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x\,\Delta y}\left(F^u_{i,j} - F^u_{i-1,j} + F^v_{i,j} - F^v_{i,j-1}\right)$$ Because it is written in flux form, water volume is conserved exactly: what leaves one cell enters its neighbour.
  3. Advection terms use first-order upwind differences, taking the gradient from the side the flow comes from. This is stable but slightly diffusive.
Stability: Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition
$$\Delta t \le \frac{\Delta x}{\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{gH}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta t = 0.45\,\frac{\Delta x_{\min}}{\sqrt{g\,(h_{\max}+6)}} \approx 33\ \text{s}$$

Gravity waves travel at \(c=\sqrt{gH}\approx 25\) m/s in the deepest (72 m) cells. The code computes \(\Delta t\) automatically from the bathymetry, which gives about 2,600 steps per simulated day.

6. Wetting & drying

With tides of up to ~10 m, large mud flats flood and dry each cycle. Three numerical safeguards keep the depth non-negative:

  • Face depth \(= \max(\eta_L,\eta_R) + \min(h_L,h_R)\). If it is below 0.10 m, the face is closed (velocity = 0).
  • Upwind flux depth: water can only leave a cell using the depth that cell actually has.
  • Positivity limiter: if a cell's total outflow in one step would exceed 80 % of its volume, all its outflows are scaled down proportionally. This guarantees \(H \ge 0\).

7. Boundary conditions

  • Land: no normal flow, i.e. \(u = 0\) or \(v = 0\) on any face touching land.
  • Open sea (south & west edges): a Dirichlet (clamped) condition, \(\eta = \eta_{\text{Open-Meteo}}(x,t)\).
    • Linear interpolation in time between the hourly values.
    • Inverse-distance weighting in space from the 8 forcing points.
    • A 4-hour ramp from rest to avoid a start-up shock, plus an 18-hour spin-up before any requested time.

8. Pollutant transport: advection–reaction equation

For each concentration field \(C\) (toxic, organic, water age):

$$\frac{\partial (HC)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (HuC)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (HvC)}{\partial y} = S - k\,H\,C$$
FieldSource \(S\)Decay \(k\)
Toxic / persistentindustry load (kg/day → g/s)0
Organicindustry load\(\ln 2 / T_{1/2}\), with \(T_{1/2}\) set by a slider
Water age\(H\) (age grows 1 day per day)0; \(C=0\) at the open boundary

Discretized as a donor-cell finite volume that reuses the same water fluxes as the continuity step:

$$(HC)^{n+1}_{i,j} = (HC)^n_{i,j} - \frac{\Delta t}{A_{i,j}} \sum_{\text{faces}} F\, C_{\text{upwind}} + S\,\Delta t$$

Water and pollutant are moved by identical fluxes, so the mass budget closes exactly: released = still in gulf + flushed to sea + degraded. The stats panel reports these four numbers, and a test run showed 246.6 t in the gulf out of 246.8 t released, with the rest flushed.

9. Particles: drifters, plumes and oil

Each particle solves a stochastic ordinary differential equation:

$$d\mathbf x = \big[\mathbf u(\mathbf x,t) + \alpha\,\mathbf W\big]\,dt + \sqrt{2K}\, d\mathbf B_t$$
  • \(\mathbf u(\mathbf x,t)\): bilinear interpolation of the C-grid velocities at the particle's position.
  • Deterministic part integrated with 2nd-order Runge–Kutta (midpoint). The random part uses the Euler–Maruyama scheme, \(\Delta\mathbf x_{\text{rand}} = \mathcal N(0,1)\sqrt{2K\Delta t}\), with \(K = 5\,\mathrm{m^2/s}\).
  • Windage \(\alpha = 0.03\) for oil, 0 otherwise. Oil and plume particles are removed at a constant rate (evaporation / ageing).
  • Drifters store one trail point per hour. The trails show tidal excursions of 10–20 km back and forth with only a small net drift, which is the "water stuck in loops" effect.

10. Residual (tide-averaged) circulation

$$\langle \mathbf u\rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_{t}^{t+T}\mathbf u\,dt, \qquad T = 2\,T_{M2} = 24.84\ \text{h}, \qquad \zeta = \frac{\partial \langle v\rangle}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial \langle u\rangle}{\partial y}$$

Averaging over two principal lunar (M2) cycles cancels the back-and-forth tidal motion. What remains is the slow residual circulation. Its vorticity \(\zeta\) is the "Residual eddies" layer: closed loops are where water and pollution tend to stay trapped.

11. Running it in the browser

  • Frame budget: each animation frame runs as many model steps as fit in ~24 ms, depending on the chosen speed (e.g. 1 h/s ≈ 110 steps per second). One step takes ~0.5 ms.
  • Drawing: the chosen field is drawn as an image at grid resolution, scaled with smoothing, with land on top. Then flow streaks, particles, industries and labels are drawn.
  • Jumping in time: the hydrodynamics restart at rest 18 h before the target and spin up quickly, while pollution fields are kept.
  • Video export: frames are rendered at a fixed amount of simulated time per frame, encoded to H.264 with WebCodecs, and written to MP4 by a small built-in muxer.

14. Limitations

  • Numerical diffusion: first-order upwind advection spreads plumes more than in reality.
  • Resolution: the 2 km grid under-resolves narrow channels and tidal creeks. This is the main reason amplification inside the gulf is ~75 % of real.
  • Depth-averaged: there is no vertical structure, so no stratification and no difference between surface and bottom currents.
  • Forcing: the boundary sea level comes from a global model (SMOC), not tide-gauge measurements.
  • Industry loads are illustrative, not measured discharges. This is an educational / exploratory tool, not for regulatory use.

Operational coastal models such as ADCIRC, Delft3D, MIKE 21 and TELEMAC solve the same equations. They add unstructured meshes, higher-order schemes, 3-D layers and careful calibration.
